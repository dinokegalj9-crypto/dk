# 07 — Photography Guidelines

> We do not photograph perfume. We photograph the *state* — the light, the texture, the residue
> of a memory. The bottle is almost never the subject.

## 1. Principles

- **Atmosphere over product.** An image's job is to trigger a feeling, not to display an object.
  If a frame could appear in a perfume ad, reshoot it.
- **Memory looks like this:** imperfect, partial, low-lit, slightly out of reach. We embrace
  blur, grain, shadow, and the edge-of-frame. Clarity is not the goal; *recognition* is.
- **Restraint.** Few images, each carrying real weight. One unforgettable frame beats a gallery
  of pretty ones. Imagery is used *sparingly* against space and type.

## 2. Subject

What we photograph, in order of preference:

1. **Light and air** — sun through a curtain, dust in a sunbeam, fog, steam on glass, the blue
   of dusk, headlights through rain. The state, rendered as atmosphere.
2. **Texture and surface** — fabric, skin, water, weathered wood, condensation, peeling paint,
   crumpled paper. The tactile residue of memory.
3. **The human, partially** — a wrist, the back of a neck, hair, an eye half-closed, a body
   turning away. Never a face selling to camera; never a posed "model with bottle." Anonymous,
   intimate, caught rather than staged.
4. **The object, rarely and reverently** — the bottle as a quiet still life, in deep shadow,
   as if found, never as hero packshot. (Packshots exist only where commerce strictly needs
   them — see §6.)

## 3. Light

- **Low, directional, natural.** Single-source light, deep shadows, generous darkness. We are a
  near-black brand (see [UI](./03-ui-guidelines.md)); imagery lives in the same low-light world.
- **Golden, blue, or grey** — the light of late afternoon, dusk, overcast, or a single lamp.
  Each collection's state suggests its own light temperature.
- **No bright, even, commercial studio light.** No ring-light gloss. No hard product-shot
  highlights. Shadow is where memory hides; protect it.

## 4. Treatment

- **Grain is mandatory, subtle to pronounced.** Digital cleanliness reads as "product." Film
  grain (or faithful emulation) evokes memory, photographs, the past. It also ties imagery to
  the UI's grain layer for one coherent world.
- **Muted, desaturated palette**, tuned toward the collection's accent. Color is felt, not
  vivid. Never oversaturated, never "punchy."
- **Soft focus, motion blur, and shallow depth** are tools, not flaws. Let parts of the frame
  dissolve.
- **Embrace imperfection** — light leaks, vignetting, lens flare, dust. Authentic over polished.
- **Consistency:** all imagery shares one grade so the site feels like a single remembered
  world, not a stock library. Define and apply a single LUT/grade per collection.

## 5. Composition

- **Asymmetry and negative space** (echoing [UI §4](./03-ui-guidelines.md)). Subject off-center,
  often small in a large quiet frame. The emptiness is the feeling.
- **Edge-of-frame and crop into intimacy** — get close, cut off, let the viewer's mind complete
  the rest. Memory is partial; the crop honors that.
- **Stillness.** Even when there's motion blur, the *mood* is held breath. No dynamic action,
  no energy-drink kineticism.

## 6. Commerce reality (the packshot problem)

Some commerce requires a clear product image. We allow it, contained:

- Keep packshots **out of the emotional flow**; place them in the quiet, factual zone near
  Composition and practical details (see [Content §6](./06-content-guidelines.md)).
- Even these are shot with intention — deep ground, soft single light, grain, generous space —
  so they belong to the world. A clear-but-beautiful object, never a catalog cutout on white.
- Never let a packshot be the *first* or *largest* image a person meets for a fragrance.

## 7. Motion imagery (film)

- The same rules govern video: slow, low-lit, grainy, atmospheric, near-silent. Footage
  *drifts*; it never cuts fast or sells.
- All motion honors [Motion Guidelines](./04-motion-guidelines.md): long durations, no autoplay
  with sound, complete in silence, respects `prefers-reduced-motion` (offer a still frame).

## 8. Accessibility

- **Alt text is written as evocatively as body copy** (see [Content §7](./06-content-guidelines.md)).
  Describe the *feeling and scene*, not "image of bottle." e.g. *"Late sun through a thin
  curtain; dust suspended in the light."*
- Never encode essential information in an image alone.
- No flashing, no harsh strobing; respect photosensitivity.

## 9. The test

> *Could this frame trigger a memory in someone who has never seen our product — or is it just a photo of perfume?*
