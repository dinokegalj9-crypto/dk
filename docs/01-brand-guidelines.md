# 01 — Brand Guidelines

## 1. What Sensorium is

Sensorium explores **memory, emotion, perception, subconscious states, and forgotten
experiences through scent.**

We do not sell fragrance. We sell **re-entry into states people thought they had lost** — the
specific calm of rain against a window you no longer live behind; the suspended dread of a
goodbye; the gold-lit boredom of a childhood afternoon that lasted a thousand years.

Scent is the trigger. **Memory is the product.**

## 2. Positioning

| | |
|---|---|
| **Category** | We refuse the category. Not "niche perfume," not "luxury fragrance." We are a memory house. |
| **Adversary** | The department-store grid. Top/heart/base note tables. Celebrity bottles. Anything that shouts. |
| **Promise** | You will feel something you forgot you could feel. |
| **Proof** | The restraint. We withhold until intensity is earned. |

Sensorium sits where **art, psychology, and perfumery** overlap. The nearest neighbors are not
other perfume brands — they are a James Turrell room, a Wong Kar-wai frame, the silence of a
gallery at closing time.

## 3. The brand soul (non-negotiable)

1. **Scent is the trigger, memory is the product.**
2. **The interface should feel like remembering, not browsing.**
3. **Restraint is the luxury.** The most immersive experiences withhold.
4. **We describe states, not ingredients.** We name what you *become*, not what's in the vial.
5. **Silence is a material.** Negative space, slowness, and quiet are design elements, not gaps.

## 4. The "States" system

Every fragrance is built around a **state** — a precise, half-remembered emotional condition.
A state is not a mood word ("fresh," "sexy," "bold"). A state is a *situation the body
remembers*.

A valid state must pass three checks:

- **Specific** — "the hour after rain stops" beats "rain."
- **Pre-verbal** — it's felt before it's named; you recognize it before you can explain it.
- **Slightly uncomfortable or bittersweet** — pure pleasure is forgettable. Memory lives in tension.

**Examples of well-formed states:**

- *The last warm day before the cold decides to stay.*
- *Being driven home asleep as a child, half-aware, completely safe.*
- *The room after everyone has left the party.*
- *Reading a letter from someone now gone.*
- *The dread-and-thrill of the first day somewhere new.*

Each fragrance has a **State Name** (poetic, internal), a **Scent Architecture** (how it
unfolds in time), and an **Emotional Arc** (where it takes you, in order).

## 5. Naming system

- **Fragrance names** evoke the state without explaining it. One to three words. Suggestive,
  never literal. Avoid "Eau de," numbers-as-gimmick, and ingredient names as titles.
  - Good: *Undertow*, *The Long Afternoon*, *Threshold*, *After You Left*, *Static*.
  - Bad: *Vanilla Dream №5*, *Ocean Breeze*, *Midnight Seduction*.
- **Collections** are named as *thresholds of consciousness*: e.g. *Waking*, *Submerged*,
  *Residue*, *The Edge of Sleep*.
- **House voice** never uses the word "perfume" in primary copy. We say *fragrance*, *scent*,
  or simply the name. Ideally, we name the *state* and let the object stay implicit.

## 6. Voice in one breath

Intimate, unhurried, certain. We speak the way a person speaks when they're telling you
something true and slightly painful — quietly, without flourish, trusting you to feel it.
We never sell. We **invite recognition.** (Full voice spec: [Content Guidelines](./06-content-guidelines.md).)

## 7. What we never do

- Never use urgency ("Limited time," "Buy now," countdown timers).
- Never use exclamation marks in brand voice.
- Never describe a fragrance primarily by its ingredients.
- Never use stock-photo lifestyle imagery or smiling models holding bottles.
- Never crowd the screen. Never autoplay loud sound. Never interrupt.
- Never explain the feeling away. Trust the person to arrive at it.

## 8. The test

> *Does this make a person feel like they are remembering something — or like they are being marketed to?*

If it's marketing, it isn't Sensorium.
