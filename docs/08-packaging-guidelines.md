# 08 — Packaging Guidelines

> The packaging is where the experience leaves the screen and enters the hands. It is the
> **residue** — the physical proof that a memory was kept. It must feel like an object you'd
> never throw away.

## 1. Principles

- **The package is a vessel for a memory, not a container for liquid.** Every material decision
  asks: *does holding this feel like holding something you'd been missing?*
- **Restraint is the luxury** (the throughline of every document). Plain, heavy, quiet, perfect.
  No foil-stamped logos shouting, no excess, no plastic theater.
- **The object continues the world.** Near-black, warm, tactile, grained, low-lit in spirit —
  the [UI](./03-ui-guidelines.md) and [Photography](./07-photography-guidelines.md) world made
  physical.
- **Ritual over reveal.** We design the *sequence* of opening as carefully as a scene transition
  (see [Motion](./04-motion-guidelines.md)) — slow, deliberate, earned.

## 2. The bottle

- **Heavy, still, and quiet.** Weight signals permanence and worth. The bottle feels like a
  stone you'd keep on a windowsill, not a thing you'd discard when empty.
- **Form:** simple, archetypal, slightly imperfect — a form that feels *found* and timeless
  rather than designed-to-impress. Frosted, smoked, or deeply tinted glass over clear; the
  liquid is glimpsed, not displayed.
- **Surface:** matte, soft-touch, with subtle tactile grain where possible. It should ask to
  be held.
- **Labeling:** minimal. The **state name** in the serif voice (see
  [Typography](./05-typography-guidelines.md)), the house mark small and quiet, debossed or
  printed low-contrast — felt before read. No ingredient lists crowding the face (those go on
  the outer, per regulation and [Content §6](./06-content-guidelines.md)).
- **The cap/closure** has a considered, weighted action — a soft, decisive *seat* when closed.
  The sound and feel of closing it is part of the design.

## 3. The outer

- **Rigid, substantial, protective** — a box that feels like a book or a keepsake case, not
  retail cardboard. Uncoated or soft-touch stock, deep tonal color, minimal printing.
- **Opening is a ritual:** a slow, resisted slide or a lid that lifts with gentle air, not a
  tear-and-toss. Magnets, snug tolerances, and a moment of darkness before the object is
  revealed — like closing your eyes between memories.
- **Inside:** the bottle nested in a soft, dark bed. A single printed card — the **state**, set
  like a passage from a book (the same copy voice as [Content](./06-content-guidelines.md)),
  perhaps a line meant to be read *before* the first spray.
- **No clutter:** no promotional inserts, no QR-code-deals, no plastic windows, no excess
  tissue theater. Every element earns its place against silence.

## 4. The first spray (the offline threshold)

The unboxing is the physical equivalent of the digital **Threshold** (see
[UX §2](./02-ux-guidelines.md)). Design it as a guided, wordless-but-for-one-line moment:

1. Weight in the hands.
2. The resisted opening; a beat of darkness.
3. The object, found in shadow.
4. The card — one line — read before the first spray.
5. The scent. The state arrives.

## 5. Material & sustainability (honest, not performative)

- **Refillable by design.** The heavy vessel is meant to be kept and refilled — refills arrive
  in minimal, low-impact packaging. Permanence is both an emotional and an ecological stance.
- **Real materials, honestly sourced** — glass, paper, metal; minimal or no single-use plastic.
  Recyclable/recycled where it doesn't compromise the keepsake quality.
- **No greenwashing.** Any sustainability claim is specific and substantiated (see
  [Content §6](./06-content-guidelines.md)). Quiet truth over loud virtue.
- Restraint serves sustainability: less material, kept longer, is the most luxurious and the
  most responsible choice at once.

## 6. System & consistency

- All collections share the system; **collection identity shows in the single accent tone and
  the state**, not in redesigned structures. One coherent house object, many memories.
- Typography, marks, and tone match the digital world exactly — a person should feel the
  *same brand* in the hand and on the screen, with no seam.

## 7. Regulatory honesty

- Required information (ingredients, allergens/IFRA, volume, batch, warnings) is present and
  compliant — placed on the outer or a discreet base, legible, never hidden. Atmosphere never
  overrides the person's right to know what they're putting on their skin.

## 8. The test

> *Empty, would someone keep this object anyway — because throwing it away would feel like throwing away a memory?*
